# The problem

Background music is everywhere: schools, hospitals, offices, gyms, waiting rooms. Someone set up a
playlist or a stream once and then walked away. The station runs, but it is just noise — no
personality, no context, no interaction. It is not a radio station, it is a speaker nobody turned off.

Platforms like RadioKing and AzuraCast solve part of this well: they give an operator infrastructure, a
virtual studio where a human can manage the air. But the moment that human steps away it goes static.
There is no intelligence behind it.

# What Mixpla adds

Mixpla is the layer **above** the infrastructure. It does not replace the stream, it makes it alive.

The platform runs AI DJ personas around the clock. They sequence the music, answer listener chat in
real time, inject contextual content — weather, news, the time of day — and shift the mood of the
station without anyone touching a button. A hospital at 3am should not sound like the same hospital at
midday; the system knows that so the operator does not have to.

# How the music works

Operators upload their own music: independent tracks, AI-generated songs, community recordings. Each
upload is analysed automatically for genre, mood, BPM and energy, so the catalog arrives labelled
rather than needing to be tagged by hand. The AI DJ then curates from that labelled catalog on the fly.

Listeners can also like or dislike what they hear, so a station learns what its audience actually wants
without anyone manually curating it.

For operators who want deeper control there is a scripting layer, where they define exactly how the
station behaves at different times of day, for different audiences and in different circumstances.

# Who it is for

Anyone running an audio environment: schools, hospitals, offices, gyms and fitness studios, waiting
rooms — and individual creators, independent artists and producers who have a large catalog and nowhere
to send it.

# Why now, and why the incumbents can't follow

The cost of making music has collapsed. Independent producers and AI tools are generating enormous
volumes of it, so the hard problem is no longer creation but **intelligent distribution and curation**,
which nobody has built infrastructure for.

Spotify cannot move here: it is locked into large label agreements and cannot champion independent or
AI-generated music without threatening those relationships. Mixpla has no catalogue obligations, so it
is free to go where the music is going.

# Business model

A subscription, with tiers that scale up to advanced scripting and multi-station management. The same
model serves an individual creator, a school, a hospital and a fitness studio. Mixpla also takes a
share of advertising revenue for stations that monetise through the platform.

# The close

Music has always been about emotion. What is changing is the economics — who makes it, what it costs,
and how it reaches people. Creation is solved; distribution and curation are not. That is the gap
Mixpla fills, for the school, the hospital, the gym, and the producer with ten thousand tracks and
nowhere to send them.
